How a rule is built

Each rule in Agloe is written as a sentence on the rules page, then as code in a slow checker, then as code in a fast checker. The two checkers must give the same answers. The example is a hire car that needs a driver.

  1. The ideaA sentence in everyday words.
  2. The wordsThe same sentence on the rules page, in defined terms.
  3. The slow checkerTries every possibility, and checks each against the sentence.
  4. The fast checkerStates the sentence as logic and gives it to a solver.
Step 1

The idea

A rule starts as a sentence in everyday words.

A manual car needs somebody in it who can drive one.

On Saturday morning Alex and Sam drive to the Indiana Dunes and back in a hire car with a manual gearbox. The drive there leaves the hotel at 8:00 am, the drive back leaves the dunes at 11:00 am, and each takes an hour. Sam can drive a manual car, and Alex and Jo cannot. The trip is from the rules page.

  • AAlex cannot drive a manual car
  • SSam can drive a manual car
  • JJo cannot drive a manual car
Step 2

The words

On the rules page the idea is CONSTRAINT 13. It is written in terms that the vocabulary defines, and it names no car and no traveler.

The sentence is written before any code. A new rule enters in a fixed order: the page, the reference checker with its tests, the examples, and then the fast implementation.

The example in those terms

the drive to the dunes
an OPTION
driving a manual car
a CAPABILITY
somebody in the car
a PERSON who takes part

The example in a FILE

The FILE records what the drive requires and what Sam can do.

In the FILE 
{ "id": "drive-out-needs-a-driver", "option": "drive-out", "requires": { "capability": "drive-manual", "of": "one" } },
In the report

drive-out-needs-a-driver: Drive to the dunes requires driving a manual car of one PERSON who takes part

In the FILE 
{ "id": "sam-drives-manual", "person": "sam", "capability": "drive-manual", "has": true },
In the report

sam-drives-manual: Sam has the CAPABILITY driving a manual car

Step 3

The slow checker

The slow checker applies the rules page directly. In the repository it is called the reference checker.

It takes one complete plan and checks it against each rule. These lines check rule 13.

It tries every possibility and uses no solver. It defines the right answer for small TRIPs.

src/agloe/reference.py

lacking = [person for person in persons if (person, capability.id) not in world.has]
if not persons or (lacking and (of == "each" or len(lacking) == len(persons))):
    named = _in_file_order_of_persons(file, lacking)
    out.append(Broken(Constraint.REQUIRED, option, named, capability=capability.id))
  1. Line 1 lists the PERSONs in the OPTION who do not have the CAPABILITY.
  2. Line 2: the rule is broken if nobody is in the OPTION, or if nobody in it has the CAPABILITY. The same line handles the rule's other case, where each PERSON must have it.
  3. Lines 3 and 4 record which rule is broken, in which OPTION, and for whom.
Step 4

The fast checker

The fast checker is the COMPILER. It does not go through the choices one by one.

It makes a yes-or-no variable for each small question about the trip: does the drive happen, is Alex in it, is Sam, is Jo.

Each rule adds statements about those variables. Rule 13 adds one statement for each CAPABILITY that an OPTION requires of one PERSON.

src/agloe/compiler.py

able = [
    n for n, person in enumerate(file.persons) if (person.id, capability) in world.has
]
if of == "one":
    model.add_bool_or(
        [self.used[index].negated(), *(self.takes[n][index] for n in able)]
    )
  1. able lists the PERSONs who have the CAPABILITY. In the example that is Sam.
  2. The statement: the OPTION does not happen, or one of those PERSONs is in it.

The two checkers must agree

The two checkers search in different ways. The same code then works out the answer and the evidence from what each search finds. If the two reports differ, one of the searches has a bug.

The first of the principles: Correct before fast. Anything faster built later must give the same answers.

One FILE The slow checkertries every possibility The fast checkerstatements and a solver the slow checker's code checks what the solver chose Report Report must be identical

  • The same reports on examples and random trips

    The CP-SAT COMPILER must give the same answers as the reference checker on every example and on random small TRIPs.

    The tests compare whole reports: the answer, the plan shown and the evidence.

  • The slow checker's code checks what the solver chose

    The solver chooses who takes part, and in what order. The slow checker's code works out the times for those choices and checks the result against the rules.

    If it finds no valid plan for them, the COMPILER stops with an error.

  • The words and the code list the same rules

    A test fails when the rules page and the slow checker name different CONSTRAINTs.

    It fails too when the rules page leaves out an example.

This is how a proof checker stands beside a prover, and how the WebAssembly specification has a reference interpreter. The decision record gives the reasons.

  8:00 am to 9:00 am: Drive to the dunes, Alex and Sam
  11:00 am to 12:00 pm: Drive back to the hotel, Alex and Sam

The plan relies on these FACTs and DECISIONs:
  drive-out-time: Drive to the dunes starts at 8:00 am on Saturday 17 July 2027
  drive-out-length: Drive to the dunes takes 1 h
  drive-out-is-open: nobody needs a right to take part in Drive to the dunes
  drive-out-needs-a-driver: Drive to the dunes requires driving a manual car of one PERSON who takes part
  drive-back-time: Drive back to the hotel starts at 11:00 am on Saturday 17 July 2027
  drive-back-length: Drive back to the hotel takes 1 h
  drive-back-arrival: nobody has to be at the Indiana Dunes before Drive back to the hotel starts
  drive-back-is-open: nobody needs a right to take part in Drive back to the hotel
  drive-back-needs-a-driver: Drive back to the hotel requires driving a manual car of one PERSON who takes part
  sam-drives-manual: Sam has the CAPABILITY driving a manual car
  lock-drive-out: Alex takes part in Drive to the dunes
  lock-drive-back: Alex takes part in Drive back to the hotel

Who meets the requirements:
  Drive to the dunes requires driving a manual car of one PERSON. Sam meets that requirement.
  Drive back to the hotel requires driving a manual car of one PERSON. Sam meets that requirement.
