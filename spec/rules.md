# Rules

This document says exactly what the COMPILER checks. It turns the principles of the [foundation](foundation.md) into CONSTRAINTs, each stated in plain words about a finished ITINERARY. Terms in capitals are defined in the [vocabulary](vocabulary.md).

The rules exist twice: here in words, and in the reference checker, [`src/agloe/reference.py`](../src/agloe/reference.py). The reference checker is a small program that applies these rules directly and uses no solver. It defines the right answer for small TRIPs, and anything faster must give the same answers. The three answers and the evidence are worked out in [`src/agloe/answers.py`](../src/agloe/answers.py), from its search or from a faster one. Every FILE under [`examples/`](../examples/) is one of its tests.

## What a FILE holds

A FILE lists PERSONs, PLACEs, OPTIONs, WAYs, WANTs, FACTs and DECISIONs. It can list BUDGETs, CAPABILITIES and OBJECTs too. Its shape is fixed by the [schema](../src/agloe/schema/file.schema.json). Every record has an id that is unique in the FILE.

A BUDGET is a named source of money. It lists the PERSONs who may draw on it for what the TRIP still has to buy, and the currencies it holds. Where one PERSON agrees to pay for another, the FILE lists the other PERSON on that BUDGET. A PERSON whom a BUDGET lists draws only on the BUDGETs that list them. The list says nothing else: not who owns the BUDGET, and not who paid before. A BUDGET can list no PERSON. It holds at least one currency, and it names no PERSON and no currency twice.

An amount of money is a decimal number written as text, with its currency beside it: `{ "amount": "32.00", "currency": "USD" }`. It has up to twelve digits, and it can have a point with one to four more after it. It has no sign, no exponent and no separator, and only an amount below one starts with a zero: `0.50`, never `032`. Two ways to write one amount are the same amount. A currency is a name of up to twenty characters, such as `USD` or `AA-points`. It starts with one of the letters A to Z or a to z. The rest are those letters, the digits 0 to 9 and hyphens. Capital and small letters differ, so `USD` and `usd` are two currencies. Amounts in different currencies are never added or compared.

An OPTION happens at one PLACE, or moves from one PLACE to another. It happens at most once, and everyone who takes part in it starts and ends together.

A WAY goes from one PLACE to another, or between two PLACEs in both directions. A PERSON can take a WAY at any time and as often as they need, alone or with others.

A WANT is something PERSONs want, or want to avoid, from the TRIP. Most WANTs are about something an OPTION can deliver: deep-dish pizza, a view from high up. A WANT can also ask that its holders end the TRIP at a PLACE it names. That WANT is about the whole ITINERARY, and no OPTION delivers it. A WANT names its holders. Each holder is a PERSON with a DEGREE OF WANT. +10 makes the WANT a must for that PERSON, and −10 makes it a must-not. A WANT names each PERSON at most once. A FILE states a WANT to end the TRIP at a PLACE only as a must. A WANT that repeats, such as dinner each night, is written as one WANT for each night, and a FACT says which OPTION SATISFIES which of them.

A CAPABILITY is something a PERSON can do, such as driving a manual car or swimming. A FILE lists it once, with a name. FACTs say which PERSONs have it and which OPTIONs require it.

An OBJECT is a physical thing that OPTIONs may require or refuse, such as a bag, a passport or a key. A FILE lists it once, with a name. At every instant it lies at one PLACE or is with one PERSON. While it is with a PERSON, it goes where that PERSON goes, on every OPTION and every WAY. An OPTION can do four things with an OBJECT, and FACTs say which OPTIONs do them.

- An OPTION can **require** an OBJECT. Then the OBJECT is **at hand** just before the OPTION starts: it lies at the PLACE where the OPTION starts, or it is with a PERSON who takes part. That is a check, and it moves nothing.
- An OPTION can **refuse** an OBJECT. Then no PERSON has the OBJECT while they take part in it. Nobody who takes part has it just before the OPTION starts, and nobody takes it there. A museum lets no large bag into the galleries. The OBJECT may lie at the PLACE where the OPTION starts: a bag outside the galleries breaks nothing. That is a check too, and it moves nothing.
- An OPTION can **hand an OBJECT over**. If the OBJECT is at hand, a PERSON who takes part can have it from the start of the OPTION. A hotel desk releases the bags, or Jo passes them to Alex over coffee. Whether anybody takes the OBJECT is a choice of the travelers.
- An OPTION can **keep an OBJECT**. If a PERSON who takes part has the OBJECT, it lies at the PLACE where the OPTION starts from then on. A museum makes Alex check the bags. That is no choice.

Nothing else changes where an OBJECT is, unless something that the FILE does not list transfers it.

An OPTION can require an OBJECT and refuse it. Then the OBJECT lies at the PLACE where the OPTION starts, and nobody brings it in. An OPTION that keeps an OBJECT and refuses it never lets in the PERSON who has it, because it checks before it keeps. A writer who means a cloakroom states one OPTION that keeps the bags, and says of the galleries that they refuse them.

A FACT states one thing:

| A FACT states | Meaning |
|---|---|
| a fixed start | The OPTION can start only at that time. |
| a start at any time | The planners choose when the OPTION starts. |
| a duration | The OPTION takes exactly that long. |
| a CONTINGENT duration | The world decides how long the OPTION takes, between a shortest and a longest value. |
| an access cutoff | Each PERSON must reach the PLACE where the OPTION starts at least that long before it starts. |
| how long a WAY takes | Each time a PERSON takes the WAY, it takes that long. The FACT gives an exact value, or a CONTINGENT range from which the world decides each time. |
| a COMPLETE set of travel | The FILE lists every OPTION and WAY that goes straight from one PLACE to another and starts within a stated span of time. Straight means that it stops at no other PLACE of the FILE on the way. So no unlisted WAY does. The span includes both of its ends. The FACT can leave out the first PLACE, the second, or both. A PLACE it leaves out stands for every PLACE. The FACT says nothing about travel through a third PLACE as a whole. Each leg of such travel is straight travel by itself, and the FACT rules out a leg only where it covers that leg. |
| that an OPTION SATISFIES a WANT | The OPTION delivers what the WANT describes. The FACT gives a DEGREE OF SATISFACTION, a whole number from 0 to 10. Zero says that the OPTION does not deliver it. Any value above zero says that it does. Only an OPTION SATISFIES a WANT. A WAY never does. |
| a COMPLETE set for a WANT | The FILE states every OPTION that SATISFIES the WANT. Each one is listed, with a FACT that it SATISFIES the WANT. So an OPTION with no FACT about the WANT does not SATISFY it, and nothing outside the FILE does. |
| where a PERSON starts from | The PERSON's path begins at that PLACE. |
| whether an OPTION needs a right | A PERSON can take part in the OPTION only with a right to it, such as a ticket, a seat or a reservation. Or taking part needs no right. |
| a BOOKING | One agreement that secures a right to one OPTION for the PERSONs it covers. It is one unit, it can be used at most once, and it covers those PERSONs together. It can state what it paid: its whole price, with nothing left to pay. The payment can name the BUDGET the money came from and the PERSON who paid. |
| an OFFER | A seller's proposal: rights to one OPTION can still be bought. It has a number of places. Each place is one right for one PERSON. The seller offers it to anybody on the TRIP, so any PERSON who can pay for it can take it. The places of two OFFERs are different places, and the seller lets all of them be taken together. An OFFER can state its price: what one place costs. It states its prices as a list, and the list has one price. |
| a COMPLETE set of the rights to an OPTION | The BOOKINGs in the FILE secure every right to the OPTION that anybody on the TRIP holds. Nobody on the TRIP can get another, except by a place of an OFFER that the FACT names. |
| whether a PERSON has a CAPABILITY | The PERSON can do it, or cannot. |
| what an OPTION requires of a CAPABILITY | The OPTION requires it of one PERSON: it can happen only if a PERSON who takes part in it has the CAPABILITY. Or it requires it of each PERSON: it can happen only if every PERSON who takes part in it has the CAPABILITY. Or it does not require it. |
| a COMPLETE set for a CAPABILITY | The FILE states every one of its OPTIONs that requires the CAPABILITY. Each has a FACT that it does. So an OPTION of the FILE with no FACT about the CAPABILITY does not require it. The FACT says nothing about who has the CAPABILITY, and nothing about travel or an OPTION that the FILE does not list. |
| where an OBJECT starts | When the TRIP starts, a PERSON has the OBJECT, or it lies at a PLACE. |
| whether an OPTION requires an OBJECT | The OPTION can happen only if the OBJECT is at hand just before it starts. Or the OPTION does not require it. |
| whether an OPTION refuses an OBJECT | No PERSON has the OBJECT while they take part in the OPTION. Or the OPTION does not refuse it. |
| what an OPTION does with an OBJECT | It hands the OBJECT over, or it keeps it, or it leaves it alone. |
| a COMPLETE set of an OBJECT's requirements | The FILE states every one of its OPTIONs that requires the OBJECT, and every one that refuses it. Each has a FACT that it does. So an OPTION of the FILE with no FACT that it requires the OBJECT does not require it, and one with no FACT that it refuses the OBJECT does not refuse it. |
| a COMPLETE set of an OBJECT's transfers | The FILE states every OPTION that hands the OBJECT over or keeps it. Each is in the FILE, with a FACT that says so. So an OPTION of the FILE with no FACT about that leaves the OBJECT alone, and nothing outside the FILE transfers it. |

A FILE states each FACT once. An OPTION has at most one start, one duration and one access cutoff, and one FACT about each WANT. A WAY has at most one duration. A WANT has at most one FACT that its set is COMPLETE. A PERSON has at most one FACT about where they start from. A FACT that an OPTION SATISFIES a WANT, and a COMPLETE set for a WANT, are about a WANT that an OPTION can deliver. An OPTION has at most one FACT about whether it needs a right, and one that its rights are COMPLETE. It can have several BOOKINGs and several OFFERs. A BOOKING covers each PERSON at most once, and two BOOKINGs can cover the same PERSON for the same OPTION. If a payment names a BUDGET, the FILE lists it and it holds the currency of the payment. If it names a PERSON who paid, the FILE lists that PERSON. The BUDGET need not list the PERSON who paid or the PERSONs the BOOKING covers, and the PERSON who paid need not be one of those: a payment that was made counts, whoever it was for. A BOOKING that is only partly paid leaves the payment out. An OFFER has at least one place, and fewer than a thousand million. A COMPLETE set of rights names every OFFER that the FILE has for its OPTION: a FILE with another OFFER for that OPTION is refused, because the two FACTs disagree. A PERSON has at most one FACT about each CAPABILITY, and so has an OPTION. A CAPABILITY has at most one FACT that its set is COMPLETE. Each of these FACTs names a CAPABILITY that the FILE lists. An OBJECT has at most one FACT about where it starts, one that the set of its requirements is COMPLETE, and one that the set of its transfers is COMPLETE. An OPTION has at most one FACT about whether it requires each OBJECT, one about whether it refuses each, and one about what it does with each. Each of these FACTs names an OBJECT that the FILE lists.

The COMPILER takes each OFFER at its word. So whoever writes the FILE records the same places once, even when two sellers offer them. After a purchase they record the BOOKING and lower the places of the OFFER. An OFFER with no place left is taken out of the FILE, and out of the COMPLETE set that names it.

A time states its date and time to the second, its offset from UTC and the name of its time zone. The offset must be the one that time zone has at that moment. Every time lies in the years 0001 to 9999, on its own clock and in UTC, and a report can be written only when every time in it does too. The COMPILER pins one release of the time-zone database, so the same FILE gets the same reading on every machine.

No text in a FILE holds a control character, a line break or half of a character pair. A name holds at least one character that is not a space.

A DECISION is a LOCK, or it says when the TRIP starts, or when the TRIP ends, or it caps what the TRIP spends from one BUDGET in one currency.

- A LOCK names an OPTION and the PERSONs who take part in it.
- A FILE holds at most one DECISION about when the TRIP starts and one about when it ends, and the TRIP does not end before it starts.
- A cap names a BUDGET that the FILE lists, and an amount in a currency that the BUDGET holds. A FILE holds at most one cap for a BUDGET and a currency. A currency with no cap is unlimited in that BUDGET. The cap is for the whole TRIP, with what is already paid.

## What is unknown

A FACT that the FILE does not state is unknown, unless a COMPLETE set settles it. The COMPILER never fills one in.

- With no stated start, an OPTION might start only at some time nobody has recorded.
- With no stated duration, an OPTION or a WAY might take any length of time.
- With no stated access cutoff, an OPTION might need any margin.
- Travel that the FILE does not list might exist between any two PLACEs, and take any length of time. The rules call it an unlisted WAY. It stands for a WAY, or for an OPTION that leaves at some time, so it can be there at one time and not at another. Where it stands for an OPTION, nobody knows which WANTs it SATISFIES, except that it SATISFIES no WANT whose set is COMPLETE and no WANT about the whole ITINERARY. A COMPLETE set of travel says that none goes straight from its first PLACE to its second within its span.
- With no FACT about an OPTION and a WANT that an OPTION can deliver, nobody knows whether the OPTION SATISFIES the WANT, unless a FACT says that the WANT's set is COMPLETE.
- An OPTION that the FILE does not list might SATISFY any such WANT whose set is not COMPLETE. The rules call it another OPTION for the WANT. Nobody knows where it happens, when it starts or how long it takes. So nobody knows which PERSONs can take part in it.
- With no FACT about whether an OPTION needs a right, nobody knows.
- With no stated price, an OFFER might quote any one amount in any one currency, the same for each of its places.
- With no stated payment, a BOOKING might have paid any amount in any currency: the FILE gives it no upper bound. With no BUDGET named, nobody knows where the money came from: a BUDGET of the FILE, or somewhere else. With no PERSON named, nobody knows who paid. A cap can turn on what a BOOKING charged to that BUDGET in that currency. That is one unknown FACT for each BOOKING: what it charged to the BUDGETs of the FILE. A BOOKING that states a payment and its BUDGET leaves nothing unknown about it, and neither does a payment of nothing. Every BOOKING of the FILE counts once, whomever it covers and whether or not anybody uses it. Only its whole settled payment can be stated: a deposit cannot, so a payment that is left out is unknown.
- Where no BUDGET lists a PERSON, the FILE does not say who pays for them. Nobody knows whether a BUDGET can, or in which currencies.
- A right that no BOOKING in the FILE secures, and that is no place of an OFFER in the FILE, might exist, for any OPTION and any PERSON: one the PERSON already holds, or one they can still get at no cost, or at a price that they can pay and that fits the caps with everything else the TRIP pays. The rules call it another right to the OPTION for the PERSON. A FACT that the rights to the OPTION are COMPLETE says there is none.
- With no FACT about a PERSON and a CAPABILITY, nobody knows whether the PERSON has it.
- With no FACT about an OPTION and a CAPABILITY, nobody knows whether the OPTION requires it, or of whom, unless a FACT says that the CAPABILITY's set is COMPLETE.
- Nobody knows what an unlisted WAY requires where it stands for an OPTION, or what another OPTION for a WANT requires.
- With no FACT about where an OBJECT starts, nobody knows. A PERSON might have it, it might lie at any PLACE, or it might be somewhere the FILE does not name.
- With no FACT about whether an OPTION requires an OBJECT, nobody knows, unless a FACT says that the set of the OBJECT's requirements is COMPLETE.
- With no FACT about whether an OPTION refuses an OBJECT, nobody knows, unless a FACT says that the set of the OBJECT's requirements is COMPLETE.
- With no FACT about what an OPTION does with an OBJECT, nobody knows, unless a FACT says that the set of the OBJECT's transfers is COMPLETE. The OPTION might hand it over, keep it or leave it alone.
- Where the set of an OBJECT's transfers is not COMPLETE, something that the FILE does not list might transfer the OBJECT: a courier, a friend, a desk. The rules call it another transfer. It can bring an OBJECT to where a plan needs it, and it can take one from a PERSON before an OPTION that refuses it. The travelers can use it or leave it: it never takes an OBJECT against their plan. Nobody knows what it can do, and the COMPILER does not work that out.

A possible world is one way that all the unknown FACTs could turn out. Its travel joins only PLACEs that the FILE names: travel through anywhere else is part of one WAY between two PLACEs of the FILE. Another OPTION for a WANT can happen anywhere.

## The CONSTRAINTs

An ITINERARY says which OPTIONs of the FILE happen, which PERSONs take part in each, which WAYs each PERSON takes, and when each of these starts and ends. A PERSON's path is their OPTIONs and WAYs in the order they do them. Each time a PERSON takes a WAY counts by itself, even when others take the same WAY at the same time. At least one PERSON takes part in every OPTION that happens. An ITINERARY lists these steps in the order they happen. An earlier start comes first, and steps that start at one instant happen one after another, in the order listed. It also says each hand-over: the OBJECT, the OPTION, and the PERSON who has the OBJECT from then on. The OPTIONs, the PERSONs, the WAYs and the start times are choices. So are the hand-overs, and the order of steps that start at one instant. So is the way to pay: the OFFER for each place the ITINERARY may have to buy, and the BUDGET for each price above nothing. Each end follows from the start and the duration, so the same choices can end at different times in different possible worlds.

An ITINERARY works in a possible world when it meets every CONSTRAINT below.

1. **LOCKs are honored.** Every LOCKed OPTION happens, and the PERSONs its LOCK names take part.
2. **Each OPTION starts when it can.** An OPTION with a fixed start starts at that time. A WAY can be taken at any time.
3. **Each OPTION and WAY takes as long as it takes.** It ends at its start plus its duration. A CONTINGENT duration is one value in its range, and for a WAY it can be a different value each time.
4. **Each PERSON is in one place at a time.** A PERSON's OPTIONs and WAYs do not overlap, and each one starts at the PLACE where the one before it ended. Where a FACT says where the PERSON starts from, their path begins at that PLACE, so the first of them starts there. With no such FACT, nobody has said where the PERSON is before the TRIP, and their path begins at the PLACE where the first of them starts. OPTIONs and WAYs that take no time and share an instant happen one after another, in any order, and that order is the same for every PERSON.
5. **Each PERSON arrives in time.** A PERSON who travels to the PLACE where an OPTION starts gets there at least its access cutoff before it starts. A PERSON whose path has no movement before the OPTION was there all along, and is in time. A WAY has no access cutoff.
6. **A COMPLETE set is the whole set.** A PERSON takes an unlisted WAY only in a possible world that has it. Where a FACT says the ways from one PLACE to another are COMPLETE, no PERSON takes an unlisted WAY that goes straight from the first PLACE to the second and starts within the stated span.
7. **Every must is met.** A PERSON for whom a WANT is a must takes part in an OPTION that SATISFIES it. Only the PERSON's own taking part counts: nobody else can meet the must for them. A possible world can also have another OPTION for the WANT that the PERSON can take part in. Then the must is met as well, and the ITINERARY does not show that OPTION. A WANT to end the TRIP at a PLACE is met another way: the PERSON's path ends at that PLACE. A path ends where the last of its OPTIONs and WAYs ends. A path with nothing in it ends where it begins: at the PLACE a FACT says the PERSON starts from. With no such FACT it can begin, and so end, at any one PLACE.
8. **Every must-not is kept.** A PERSON for whom a WANT is a must-not takes part in no OPTION that SATISFIES it, and takes no unlisted WAY that does. Only the PERSON's own taking part counts: a must-not stops nobody else.
9. **Everything happens within the TRIP.** No OPTION and no WAY starts before the TRIP starts, and none ends after the TRIP ends. Where no DECISION says when the TRIP starts, or when it ends, there is no such limit.
10. **Each PERSON has the rights they need.** Where an OPTION needs a right, each PERSON who takes part in it has one. A BOOKING for the OPTION gives one to each PERSON it covers, for that OPTION only. A possible world can also have another right to the OPTION for a PERSON. Every other PERSON who takes part takes one place of an OFFER for the OPTION, and no place is taken twice. So the PERSONs who take part with no BOOKING and no other right are no more than the places of the OPTION's OFFERs. Only the PERSON's own right counts, and a right puts nobody into an OPTION: a PERSON whom a BOOKING covers can stay away. The COMPILER checks rights to OPTIONs only, and none to a WAY.
11. **Each place is paid for.** A place of an OFFER that the ITINERARY still buys at a price above nothing is paid from one BUDGET. That BUDGET lists the PERSON who takes the place, and it holds the currency of the price. Each place is paid for by itself, so two places that one PERSON takes can be paid from two BUDGETs. A place that costs nothing draws on no BUDGET, and anybody can take it. A possible world can have a BUDGET that pays for a PERSON whom no BUDGET of the FILE lists, whatever they take.

12. **No cap is passed.** For each cap: what the BOOKINGs of the FILE charged to that BUDGET in that currency, plus the prices of the places that the ITINERARY pays from it in that currency, is no more than the cap. Where a FILE has no cap, this CONSTRAINT asks nothing.
13. **Each requirement of an OPTION is met.** Where an OPTION requires a CAPABILITY of one PERSON, a PERSON who takes part in it has that CAPABILITY. Where it requires it of each PERSON, every PERSON who takes part in it has it. One PERSON can meet several requirements, and two PERSONs can meet different ones. Only a PERSON who takes part counts, and they take part like everybody else: with their own path to the OPTION, in time for its access cutoff, with a right where it needs one, and from its start to its end. The COMPILER checks what the OPTIONs of the FILE require, and nothing about a WAY. Where a FILE lists no CAPABILITY, this CONSTRAINT asks nothing.
14. **Each OBJECT is at hand where it is required or handed over.** Where an OPTION requires an OBJECT, the OBJECT is at hand just before the OPTION starts: it lies at the PLACE where the OPTION starts, or it is with a PERSON who takes part. The same holds where an OBJECT is handed over in an OPTION. An OBJECT is handed over only in an OPTION that hands it over, and to a PERSON who takes part. Where an OBJECT is follows from where it starts, from each OPTION that keeps it, and from each hand-over, in the order the ITINERARY lists its steps. Each step first checks what it requires and what it refuses. Then it keeps what it keeps, and then OBJECTs are handed over in it. An OPTION that keeps an OBJECT happens with or without it. Where an OBJECT is depends on no duration. The COMPILER checks what the OPTIONs of the FILE require, and nothing about a WAY. Where a FILE lists no OBJECT, this CONSTRAINT asks nothing.
15. **No PERSON has an OBJECT in an OPTION that refuses it.** Where an OPTION refuses an OBJECT, no PERSON who takes part has the OBJECT just before the OPTION starts, and the OBJECT is not handed over in it. Where the OBJECT is follows as the fourteenth CONSTRAINT says. The OBJECT may lie at the PLACE where the OPTION starts. Where a FILE lists no OBJECT, this CONSTRAINT asks nothing.

The places of an OPTION need not be alike: a PERSON may be able to pay for one and not for another. So an ITINERARY also says what gives each PERSON their right to each OPTION that needs one: a BOOKING, or a place of an OFFER with its price and the BUDGET that pays. That is its way to pay, and it is for the whole TRIP: one BUDGET can pay for places of several OPTIONs, and its cap counts them together. The way to pay is a choice, and the tenth, eleventh and twelfth CONSTRAINTs are about the same one. It is conditional in two ways. In a possible world with another right for the PERSON, that right gives it: it stands for a BOOKING or an OFFER outside the FILE, so no record names it, and the PERSON buys no place of an OFFER in the FILE. And in a possible world where the OPTION needs no right, nobody buys a place of it. A way to pay never makes anybody buy what a FACT makes unnecessary.

A fixed rule says which way to pay a report shows. A PERSON whom a BOOKING covers has the first such BOOKING in the FILE's order. The purchases go in the order of the steps of the ITINERARY shown, and within a step in the FILE's order of PERSONs. Each takes the first pair of an OFFER and a BUDGET, in the FILE's order of OFFERs and then of BUDGETs, that the PERSON can use and that leaves a way to pay for every purchase after it, in the case being checked. A pair chosen earlier stays. A place that costs nothing has one choice for its OFFER, with no BUDGET. Where a FILE has no cap, this is the rule for each OPTION by itself: the first OFFER that leaves a way for the rest, and the first BUDGET that can pay.

So a PERSON can take WAYs before their first OPTION and after their last one, and a PERSON can take WAYs without taking part in any OPTION. Where a DECISION says when the TRIP ends, a PERSON who has to end the TRIP at a PLACE is there by then: the last thing they do is over.

## The three answers

- **FEASIBLE:** one ITINERARY, with its way to pay, works in every possible world.
- **INFEASIBLE:** no ITINERARY works in any possible world.
- **UNDETERMINED:** no ITINERARY works in all of them, and the COMPILER cannot rule the TRIP out. Some ITINERARY works in some possible world, or the answer turns on another transfer of an OBJECT, which the COMPILER does not work out. The report says which.

A FEASIBLE report names the BUDGET that pays for each place at a price above nothing, and the travelers act on it. So a way to pay that would have to change with a FACT nobody knows is none. Give two BUDGETs a cap of 1 USD each. Either can pay for Sam's place at 1 USD. An old BOOKING paid 1 USD and names no BUDGET: the money came from one of the two, or from outside the FILE. Every possible world has a way to pay, and the TRIP is UNDETERMINED: no report can tell Sam which BUDGET to use.

A CONTINGENT duration has a stated range, so it is not unknown, and a possible world does not settle it. FEASIBLE may rely on any value in the range. A shorter duration is never harder, so the three answers use the shortest value of each.

Two cases settle the answer. A choice of OPTIONs, PERSONs, WAYs, start times, hand-overs and the order of the steps is a schedule. For the same schedule, every CONSTRAINT is at least as easy to meet when an OPTION can start more freely or needs less margin, when an OPTION or a WAY takes less time, when more WAYs exist, when more PERSONs can take part in another OPTION for a WANT, when fewer OPTIONs need a right, when more PERSONs have a right, when more PERSONs can pay for a place, when a BOOKING charged less to one cap and no more to any other, when an OPTION requires less of a CAPABILITY, when more PERSONs have a CAPABILITY, when fewer OPTIONs require an OBJECT, and when fewer OPTIONs refuse one. An OPTION requires the least where it requires nothing, more where it requires the CAPABILITY of one PERSON, and the most where it requires it of each PERSON: somebody takes part in every OPTION that happens, so where each of them has the CAPABILITY, one of them does. The way to pay can be another one then: a price of nothing drops its BUDGET, and a right that exists drops the purchase. Whether an OPTION SATISFIES a WANT is different: it helps a must and hurts a must-not. So each case takes it the way that suits the case. What an OPTION does with an OBJECT is different too. An OPTION that keeps the bags hurts a plan that carries them on, and helps a plan that picks them up there later. Where an OBJECT starts is the same: no one start suits every plan.

- **The best case.** Every unknown start is free, and every unknown duration and access cutoff is zero. An unlisted WAY joins any two PLACEs, takes no time and SATISFIES no WANT, except where a COMPLETE set of travel says there is none. An OPTION with no FACT about a WANT does not SATISFY it, so it breaks no must-not. For every must on a WANT that an OPTION can deliver, and whose set is not COMPLETE, there is another OPTION that the PERSON can take part in whatever else they do. It SATISFIES nothing else, it takes no time, and it comes before everything else the PERSON does, at the PLACE where they start. A PERSON who does nothing else can take part in it anywhere. So every such must is met here, with no help from a listed OPTION. An OPTION with no FACT about whether it needs a right needs none. Where an OPTION needs one, every PERSON has another right to it, except where a FACT says that its rights are COMPLETE. The places of every OFFER are there as well. An OFFER with no stated price quotes nothing, so anybody can take its places. A BUDGET with no cap can pay for each PERSON whom no BUDGET lists. A BOOKING charged nothing to any BUDGET of the FILE, unless it states the payment and the BUDGET: a payment that is not stated was nothing, and a payment with no BUDGET named came from outside the FILE. An OPTION with no FACT about a CAPABILITY does not require it, and a PERSON with no FACT about a CAPABILITY has it. An OPTION with no FACT about an OBJECT does not require it and does not refuse it. These hold together, so the best case is one possible world. A schedule that works with some way to pay in any possible world has a way to pay here too, so the TRIP is INFEASIBLE when nothing works here. An OBJECT adds two things. Where no FACT says where it starts, and the set of its transfers is COMPLETE, there is one best case for each start: a PERSON has it, or it lies at a PLACE, or it is somewhere the FILE does not name. The TRIP is INFEASIBLE when nothing works in any of them. Where the set of its transfers is not COMPLETE, the fourteenth and the fifteenth CONSTRAINT ask nothing of the OBJECT here: another transfer might put it at hand wherever a plan needs it, and take it away before an OPTION that refuses it. That is more than any possible world need allow, so it is an upper bound. Nothing that works in a possible world fails here.
- **The worst case.** An OPTION with an unknown start cannot be used. An unknown duration has no limit, so no PERSON does anything after that OPTION or WAY, and it is not over before the TRIP ends where the TRIP has an end. An unknown access cutoff has no limit, so only a PERSON who was at the PLACE all along is in time. No unlisted WAY exists. Where the set of a WANT that an OPTION can deliver is not COMPLETE, nobody knows whether an OPTION with no FACT about the WANT SATISFIES it. So it meets no must, and no PERSON for whom the WANT is a must-not takes part in it. That is no single possible world, but a choice that relies on such an OPTION either way fails in one. There is no other OPTION for any WANT. An OPTION with no FACT about whether it needs a right needs one. There is no other right to any OPTION: only the BOOKINGs and the places of the OFFERs. An OFFER with no stated price might quote in a currency that no BUDGET of the PERSON holds, so nobody can count on a place of it. A PERSON whom no BUDGET lists can count only on a place that costs nothing. Each cap is tested by itself, with everything that might have been charged to it: the payments that name its BUDGET in its currency, every payment in its currency that names no BUDGET, and the purchases of the way to pay, needed or not. Where a BOOKING states no payment, no cap can be shown to hold. An OPTION with no FACT about a CAPABILITY requires it of each PERSON who takes part, unless a FACT says that the CAPABILITY's set is COMPLETE: then it does not require it. A PERSON with no FACT about a CAPABILITY does not have it. An OPTION with no FACT about whether it requires an OBJECT requires it, and one with no FACT about whether it refuses an OBJECT refuses it, unless a FACT says that the set of the OBJECT's requirements is COMPLETE. An OBJECT with no FACT about where it starts might be anywhere: with any PERSON, at any PLACE, or somewhere the FILE does not name. A plan has to hold wherever it is, so all of those places are followed at once, through the same steps. Such an OBJECT is at hand for no OPTION, because it might be somewhere the FILE does not name. It is in an OPTION that refuses it wherever a PERSON who takes part might have it. An OPTION that keeps it clears whoever takes part: they do not have it afterwards, whatever nobody knew before. Where the set of an OBJECT's transfers is not COMPLETE, an OPTION with no FACT about what it does with the OBJECT hands nothing over, and nobody knows whether it keeps the OBJECT. So where a PERSON who takes part has the OBJECT, it is afterwards with that PERSON or at the PLACE where the OPTION starts, and a plan has to hold with both. There is no other transfer: a plan that works without one works with one too, because the travelers can leave it. Those sums need not all be true together. But one ITINERARY with its way to pay works here exactly when it works in every possible world, so the TRIP is FEASIBLE when something works here.

So a must on a WANT that an OPTION can deliver can make a TRIP INFEASIBLE only where a FACT says that the WANT's set is COMPLETE. Otherwise another OPTION might be found, and the must makes the TRIP UNDETERMINED when no OPTION that a FACT says SATISFIES the WANT fits. A must-not can make a TRIP INFEASIBLE: a LOCK names a PERSON in an OPTION that a FACT says SATISFIES what they must not have.

A must to end the TRIP at a PLACE makes a TRIP UNDETERMINED where the PERSON still has to travel there and no listed OPTION or WAY gets them there in time: other WAYs might. It makes a TRIP INFEASIBLE only where COMPLETE sets of travel rule those out, on every route there and at every time the PERSON could still leave. Without a DECISION about when the TRIP ends, the PERSON could always wait until a span has passed. Two such musts that name different PLACEs for one PERSON make a TRIP INFEASIBLE by themselves.

A missing right can make a TRIP INFEASIBLE only where one FACT says that the OPTION needs a right and another says that its rights are COMPLETE. Otherwise the OPTION might need none, or another right might exist, and the missing right makes the TRIP UNDETERMINED at most. Where both FACTs are there, a choice breaks the tenth CONSTRAINT when the PERSONs who take part in the OPTION with no BOOKING are more than its places on OFFER.

A cap can make a TRIP INFEASIBLE in two ways. What the BOOKINGs charged can pass it by itself, whatever the plan is. And a purchase can pass it, where the right is needed and nothing else can give it: the OPTION needs a right and its rights are COMPLETE.

A requirement can make a TRIP INFEASIBLE only where a FACT states it, and only through PERSONs whom a FACT says do not have the CAPABILITY. With no FACT, the OPTION might require nothing, and the PERSON might have the CAPABILITY, so the TRIP is UNDETERMINED at most. The requirement is checked with everything else, and not for one OPTION by itself. Two LOCKed drives whose times overlap, and that each require a driver, make a TRIP INFEASIBLE where only one PERSON can drive, though either drive could have that PERSON. A must-not, a missing right or a LOCK elsewhere at that hour can keep the one PERSON who has the CAPABILITY out of an OPTION.

An OBJECT can make a TRIP INFEASIBLE only where a FACT says that an OPTION requires it or refuses it, and another says that the set of its transfers is COMPLETE. Without the first, the OPTION might not require it, or might not refuse it. Without the second, another transfer might bring it or take it away, and the TRIP is UNDETERMINED at most. The best case is not exact there. It lets the OBJECT be at hand wherever a plan needs it, and one permit cannot be at two desks an ocean apart at one instant. So UNDETERMINED can be said of a TRIP that no transfer could save. FEASIBLE and INFEASIBLE are never said wrongly. A writer gets an exact answer by stating that the set is COMPLETE.

So a writer who lists a CAPABILITY has more to state. Once a FILE lists driving a manual car and says nothing more, a LOCKed lunch that was FEASIBLE is UNDETERMINED: the lunch might require it, and nobody is known to have it. One FACT settles that for every OPTION of the FILE: the CAPABILITY's set is COMPLETE. An OBJECT is the same. Once a FILE lists the bags and says that Alex has them, a visit that Alex is LOCKed into might require them, it might refuse them, and it might keep them. Two FACTs settle that for every OPTION of the FILE: both sets of the OBJECT are COMPLETE.

## ROBUST

An ITINERARY is ROBUST when it works, with its way to pay, in every possible world, for every value of every CONTINGENT duration. A longer duration is never easier, so it is enough to check the longest value of each.

A FEASIBLE TRIP with no ROBUST ITINERARY gets a WARNING. The WARNING names each CONSTRAINT that the ITINERARY shown would break, with its steps in the order shown, if every CONTINGENT duration ran longest, with the OPTION or the time a WAY is taken, the PERSONs and how late they would be. No duration changes who takes part in what or where a path ends, so a longer duration never leaves a must unmet or breaks a must-not, and this WARNING names only CONSTRAINTs about time.

## Not booked yet

A place of an OFFER is not secured until it is bought. Where the ITINERARY shown takes a place of an OFFER, the report gives a WARNING. It names each PERSON who still has to buy a right, the OPTION, the OFFER the place is from and its price. Where the price is above nothing, it names the BUDGET that pays. Where nobody knows whether the OPTION needs a right, the WARNING says that the right has to be bought only if the OPTION needs one. A BOOKING for that OPTION that covers the PERSON ends the WARNING about them for that OPTION.

This WARNING is about the ITINERARY shown, like the first one. A report can give both.

## Which ITINERARY is shown

A report shows an ITINERARY that the travelers can use only for a FEASIBLE TRIP. That ITINERARY works in the worst case, where no unlisted WAY exists, so it takes only WAYs that the FILE lists. In a FILE with a cap, an UNDETERMINED report also shows one plan for each of its smallest sets: it works if that set turns out well. When several ITINERARIES work, the report shows one, chosen by a fixed rule:

1. a ROBUST one that takes no place of an OFFER, if there is any;
2. then a ROBUST one;
3. then one that takes no place of an OFFER;
4. then one in which PERSONs take part the fewest times, counting each PERSON once for every OPTION they take part in and once for every time they take a WAY;
5. then one with the fewest OPTIONs;
6. then one that uses the OPTIONs listed first in the FILE;
7. then one in which each PERSON does the OPTIONs listed first, in the FILE's order where they can. Going through the PERSONs in the FILE's order, and through each PERSON's OPTIONs in the order they do them, the ITINERARY with the OPTION listed first comes first. A PERSON with nothing more to do comes before one with more;
8. then one in which each PERSON takes the WAYs listed first. Going through the PERSONs in the FILE's order, and through the WAYs each PERSON takes in the order they take them, the ITINERARY with the WAY listed first comes first. An unlisted WAY comes after every listed one, and among unlisted WAYs the FILE's order of their first PLACE decides, and then of their second;
9. then one with the fewest hand-overs;
10. then one whose hand-overs come first. Going through the OBJECTs in the FILE's order, and through the hand-overs of each in the order they happen, the ITINERARY with the OPTION listed first comes first, and then the one with the PERSON listed first. An OBJECT with no more hand-overs comes before one with more;
11. then one whose steps come first. Going through the steps in the order the ITINERARY lists them, the ITINERARY with the earlier start comes first, then the one with an OPTION before a WAY, then the FILE's order of OPTIONs, of WAYs, of PERSONs and of PLACEs.

Rules 9 to 11 choose among hand-overs and among orders of the steps. Each order has the start times that the rule further on gives it. These rules decide only where an OPTION of the ITINERARY requires or refuses an OBJECT.

Where several OPTIONs could meet a must, the same rules choose among them. The DEGREE OF SATISFACTION plays no part: any value above zero meets a must and breaks a must-not.

An ITINERARY takes no place of an OFFER when it works with every place left unspent. So a plan that is sure to hold in time comes before a plan that is fully booked and can run late: a purchase is in the travelers' hands, and a late train is not. No rule asks for the fewest places among the ITINERARIES that take some.

The fourth rule keeps out anything nobody needs. If any OPTION were taken out of the ITINERARY shown, or any PERSON out of one of its OPTIONs, or any WAY a PERSON takes, it would stop working in some possible world, or stop being ROBUST. So no PERSON in it goes round in a circle by WAYs alone: they could wait where they are and take fewer WAYs.

The order of the steps follows a fixed rule. An ITINERARY lists a step after every step that has to come before it. Among the steps that are free to come next, the first is the one with the earliest start, then an OPTION before a WAY, then the FILE's order of OPTIONs, of WAYs, of PERSONs and of PLACEs. Where an OPTION of the ITINERARY requires or refuses an OBJECT, the order matters more: where the OBJECT is turns on it, also among OPTIONs of different PERSONs that start at one instant. So the OPTIONs that require that OBJECT, refuse it or can move it have an order of their own, and it is a choice. An OPTION can move it where it hands it over, or where it keeps it and a PERSON who takes part can have it. An OPTION that refuses it hands it over to nobody. A PERSON can have it who has it when the TRIP starts, or might, or who takes part in an OPTION of the ITINERARY that hands it over. An OPTION that might hand it over or keep it counts as one that does. Every such order that keeps each PERSON's own order is tried. An OBJECT that no OPTION of the ITINERARY requires or refuses puts no OPTION in that order. Neither does an OBJECT that no OPTION of the ITINERARY can move: it stays where it starts, whatever the order. An order that goes round in a circle is no order, also where every step in it starts at one instant. Two bags lie on a desk. At 10:00 am Alex has to see the blue one and takes the red one, and Sam has to see the red one and takes the blue one. Whoever comes first takes the bag that the other has to see, so that pair of hand-overs does not work.

The start times follow a fixed rule too. It treats each time a PERSON takes a WAY as an OPTION of its own. Here one OPTION comes before another when a PERSON does it earlier, when the order of the last paragraph puts it earlier, or when a chain of such OPTIONs links the two. An OPTION that the order puts earlier starts no later. At first, only the OPTIONs with a fixed start are timed.

1. An OPTION that comes after one already timed starts as early as it can.
2. Then an OPTION that comes before one already timed starts as late as it can.
3. Steps 1 and 2 repeat until they time nothing more.
4. The OPTIONs still without a time are tied to no fixed start. They are timed from the time the TRIP starts, each as early as it can. Where no DECISION says when the TRIP starts, they are timed from the first second of 1970 in UTC, or from earlier where the TRIP ends too soon for that.

The times of the TRIP are limits, and a FILE can hold either, both or neither. They are no fixed starts: steps 1 to 3 time nothing from them. "As early as it can" and "as late as it can" keep to them, for the OPTION itself and for whatever comes before or after it: a start that leaves no room for the rest inside the TRIP is not one the OPTION can take.

A COMPLETE set of travel can rule an unlisted WAY out for a span of time. So an ITINERARY with such a WAY is timed as above, and then again with one more limit on when the WAY starts: before the first span that rules it out, and after each of those spans. A limit that leaves the WAY no time to start gives no timing. A limit before a span can make step 4 count from earlier than the first second of 1970, as the end of the TRIP can. The first of these timings that works is the timing of the ITINERARY. Where it has several such WAYs, they are taken in the FILE's order of PERSONs, and for each PERSON in the order they take them. Every combination of their limits is gone through in turn, with the last WAY changing first.

A ROBUST ITINERARY shows each CONTINGENT duration at its longest value, and any other ITINERARY shows it at its shortest. An OPTION or a WAY with an unknown duration shows no end.

## Evidence

- **FEASIBLE:** the ITINERARY, and the FACTs, WANTs and DECISIONs it relies on. These are every FACT about an OPTION or a WAY in the ITINERARY, every WANT, every LOCK and every cap. FACTs about rights, FACTs about CAPABILITIES and FACTs about OBJECTs have their own rules, further on in this paragraph. Where the ITINERARY has anything in it, they include the DECISIONs about when the TRIP starts and ends. A FACT about where a PERSON starts from is one of them where the PERSON is in the ITINERARY or has to end the TRIP at a PLACE. A FACT that a WANT's set is COMPLETE is one of them where a PERSON for whom the WANT is a must-not takes part in an OPTION with no FACT about the WANT: only the COMPLETE set says that such an OPTION does not SATISFY it. Of the FACTs about rights, the ITINERARY relies on the one about whether each of its OPTIONs needs a right, where the FILE states one. Unless that FACT says the OPTION needs none, it also relies on what gives each PERSON who takes part their right, by the fixed rule above: a BOOKING, or the OFFER whose place they take. So a BOOKING or an OFFER is named where nobody knows whether the OPTION needs a right: the plan holds either way. The ITINERARY relies on no other OFFER, and on no FACT that the rights to an OPTION are COMPLETE. It relies on no other BOOKING either, except in a FILE with a cap. There it relies on every BOOKING: each one settles or limits what it charged, and that is how each cap is known to hold. The report also says, for each PERSON, which BOOKING or OFFER it is. Where a BOOKING covers more PERSONs than the plan credits to it, the words name those it is used for. The report also states the money of the plan. First comes each BOOKING of the FILE that states a payment, whether or not the ITINERARY uses it: its amount, and the PERSON who paid and the BUDGET where it names them. Then comes each BOOKING that states no payment, so that nobody reads a sum as the whole. Then come three sums for each currency: the settled payments that the BOOKINGs state, the places that the ITINERARY shown still buys from OFFERs, and the most that it buys besides, depending on which OPTIONs turn out to need a right. The sums are exact. A payment or a price of nothing enters no sum. Neither does a deposit, or the rest owed on a BOOKING: a BOOKING states only a whole payment. The report leaves these lines out where no BOOKING states a payment and the ITINERARY buys no place at a price above nothing. In a FILE with a cap, the report gives one row for each cap, even where nothing is charged to it: its BUDGET, its currency and its limit, what the BOOKINGs charged to it for sure, each BOOKING that might have charged it besides, what the ITINERARY still buys from it, the most it buys besides if OPTIONs turn out to need a right, and what the cap has left after all of those. Of the FACTs about CAPABILITIES, the ITINERARY relies on these, for each of its OPTIONs and each CAPABILITY. Where a FACT states what the OPTION requires, it relies on that FACT. Where that FACT says the OPTION requires the CAPABILITY of one PERSON, it also relies on the FACT that one PERSON in the OPTION has it: the first who has, in the FILE's order. Where it says of each PERSON, it relies on that FACT for everybody in the OPTION. Where no FACT states what the OPTION requires and the CAPABILITY's set is COMPLETE, it relies on the COMPLETE set: only that says the OPTION requires nothing. Where the set is not COMPLETE either, it relies on the FACT that each PERSON in the OPTION has the CAPABILITY: the plan holds whatever the OPTION requires. It relies on no FACT that a PERSON does not have a CAPABILITY. The report also says who meets each requirement: the PERSONs whose FACTs the plan relies on for it. The requirements come in the order of the steps of the ITINERARY, and for each OPTION in the FILE's order of CAPABILITIES. An OPTION that requires nothing has no line. Where the FILE leaves open what an OPTION requires, the report says so. Of the FACTs about OBJECTs, the ITINERARY relies on these. For each of its OPTIONs and each OBJECT, it relies on the FACT about whether the OPTION requires the OBJECT, and on the FACT about whether it refuses it. Where the FILE states no FACT of one of those kinds and a FACT says that the set of the OBJECT's requirements is COMPLETE, it relies on that set: only that says the OPTION does not require the OBJECT, or does not refuse it. The rest is about each OBJECT that an OPTION of the ITINERARY requires or refuses, or might. The report says where the OBJECT is throughout the ITINERARY: where it starts, and from which step it is somewhere else, and why. The ITINERARY relies on each record that says so. That is the FACT about where the OBJECT starts. It is the FACT about what an OPTION does with the OBJECT, for each of its OPTIONs in which the OBJECT is handed over, and for each one in which a PERSON has the OBJECT or might. Where the FILE states no such FACT and a FACT says that the set of the OBJECT's transfers is COMPLETE, the ITINERARY relies on that set: only that says the OPTION leaves the OBJECT alone. Where the FILE has neither, the report names each place where the OBJECT might be from then on. So these records are more than the ITINERARY needs to work: some only say where the OBJECT is afterwards. Under where the OBJECT is, the report names each OPTION of the ITINERARY that requires the OBJECT or refuses it, or might, and where the OBJECT is just before that OPTION starts. Where the OBJECT might be with any PERSON, at any PLACE or somewhere the FILE does not name, the report says that nobody knows where it is.
- **INFEASIBLE:** a set of FACTs, WANTs and DECISIONs that make the TRIP INFEASIBLE by themselves, with the BUDGETs of the FILE as they are, and from which none can be removed. The PERSONs, the PLACEs, the BUDGETs, the CAPABILITIES and the OBJECTs stay as the FILE lists them while records are removed. Removing a FACT makes what it stated unknown. That is more than leaving the FACT out: where a WANT's set is COMPLETE, an OPTION whose FACT about the WANT is removed might SATISFY the WANT or not. So the set is the smallest by this test, and a FILE with only these records can be one that fewer records already rule out. The set is one conflict: a TRIP can have another, so taking a record away need not make the TRIP work. Removing a WANT removes its musts and must-nots. Removing the FACT about where a PERSON starts from lets their path begin anywhere. Removing a BOOKING makes it unknown whom the BOOKING covers and what it charged: it might cover anybody for its OPTION, and it might have charged nothing. Removing a cap lifts that limit only: the BUDGET, its other caps and every payment stay. Removing an OFFER makes its places and its price unknown: it might have a place for every PERSON at once, at no cost. Removing a FACT about a PERSON and a CAPABILITY makes it unknown whether the PERSON has it. Removing a FACT about what an OPTION requires makes that unknown for that OPTION and that CAPABILITY, also where the CAPABILITY's set is COMPLETE. Removing the COMPLETE set makes it unknown what the OPTIONs with no FACT require, and at best they require nothing, as the set said. So no conflict holds a COMPLETE set for a CAPABILITY. Where the set holds a requirement of one PERSON, the report names the PERSONs of the FILE: nobody else can come to meet it. Removing the FACT about where an OBJECT starts makes that unknown: a PERSON might have it, or it might lie at any PLACE. Removing a FACT about whether an OPTION requires an OBJECT makes that unknown, and at best the OPTION does not require it. Removing a FACT about whether an OPTION refuses an OBJECT makes that unknown, and at best the OPTION does not refuse it. So a conflict can hold a FACT that an OPTION refuses an OBJECT. Removing a FACT about what an OPTION does with an OBJECT makes that unknown for that OPTION and that OBJECT, also where the set of the OBJECT's transfers is COMPLETE: the OPTION might hand it over, keep it or leave it alone. So a conflict can hold a FACT that an OPTION hands an OBJECT over: without the FACT, the OPTION might keep it, and that can be what a plan needs. Removing the COMPLETE set of an OBJECT's transfers lets another transfer put the OBJECT at hand wherever a plan needs it, and take it away before an OPTION that refuses it. So every conflict that turns on where an OBJECT is holds that COMPLETE set, whether it is about a requirement or a refusal. No conflict holds a FACT that an OPTION does not require an OBJECT or does not refuse it, or a COMPLETE set of an OBJECT's requirements. So where a conflict leaves a PERSON without a right, it holds every BOOKING and every OFFER for the OPTION: together they show how many PERSONs can have one. The COMPILER goes through the FACTs, then the WANTs, then the DECISIONs, each in the FILE's order, and removes each record that the TRIP stays INFEASIBLE without. A set that holds a cap is about the BUDGETs as the FILE lists them, and the report prints them. It also gives the payment of each BOOKING in the set and the limit of each cap in it, in words and in the JSON. One more test follows where the set holds no cap, holds an OFFER with a price above nothing, and the FILE lists a BUDGET. It takes all the BUDGETs out at once, so that nobody knows who pays for anybody. If the set then no longer rules out every ITINERARY, the conflict turns on who can pay. The report says that the records conflict given the BUDGETs of the FILE, and prints them: the PERSONs each lists and the currencies it holds.
- **UNDETERMINED:** the unknown FACTs that would make the TRIP FEASIBLE. A set of unknown FACTs is enough when the TRIP is FEASIBLE with those FACTs turned out well and every other unknown FACT as in the worst case. A start, a length of time, other WAYs and another OPTION turn out well as in the best case. Whether an OPTION SATISFIES a WANT can help either way, so it gives two unknown FACTs: that it does, and that it does not. A set states which of the two, and that is how it turns out. A set never holds both. The report lists the sets that are enough and have the fewest FACTs. This is a choice about what a report says first: what to find out about the OPTIONs and WAYs that the FILE already lists. "Another OPTION is found for this WANT and this PERSON" is a different kind of condition. It asks for something new, and it stands in for a listed OPTION and every FACT about it. So the report first keeps the sets with the fewest of these conditions, and lists those among them with the fewest FACTs. "Another transfer of this OBJECT" is such a condition too, for an OBJECT whose set of transfers is not COMPLETE, and it is counted with the others. It differs in one way: a set that holds it is not settled. With that condition turned out well, the fourteenth and the fifteenth CONSTRAINT ask nothing of the OBJECT. The TRIP then works with the OBJECT at hand wherever the plan needs it, and out of each OPTION that refuses it. The COMPILER has not worked out whether anything can bring it there or take it away. So the report says that the TRIP might work, and that the case is not settled. It names each OPTION for which the OBJECT has to be at hand, and each OPTION that it has to stay out of, and reads those off one ITINERARY: the first, by rules 4 to 11 of "Which ITINERARY is shown", that works when the set turns out well and every other unknown FACT badly. For some of those OPTIONs, what the FILE lists may already put the OBJECT at hand in that ITINERARY, or keep it out. The report names the others first: another transfer has to put the OBJECT at hand for them, and leave it at hand for the rest. Then come the OPTIONs that refuse the OBJECT: another transfer has to keep it out of those where what the FILE lists does not, and leave it out of the rest. Where the FILE does not say whether such an OPTION requires the OBJECT, the report says that the OBJECT has to be at hand if it does. Where the FILE does not say whether it refuses the OBJECT, the report says that the OBJECT has to stay out if it does. Where one FACT says that an OPTION requires the OBJECT and another says that it refuses it, the report says that the OBJECT has to lie where the OPTION starts. In the JSON the condition lists those checks. Each has its OPTION, whether the OPTION requires or refuses the OBJECT, whether a FACT states that, and whether the check already holds with what the FILE lists. It has `"settled": false`, and that is said of its whole set, and of the plan that comes with the set in a FILE with a cap. A report lists a set that is not settled only where no settled set asks for as few new things. A settled set comes first, however many FACTs it holds: one more question about a listed OPTION is worth asking where the answer gives the travelers a plan they can use. Other WAYs count as one FACT: they stand in for one listed WAY and its duration, and the report says how long they may take. When a set holds exactly one unknown FACT, and that FACT is a length of time, the report gives the most it can be: "works if the cab takes 45 minutes or less". When any length of time would do, the report gives no limit, and the FACT only has to be stated. What an OPTION requires of a CAPABILITY turns out well where it requires nothing, and whether a PERSON has a CAPABILITY turns out well where they have it. When a set holds exactly one unknown FACT, and that FACT is what an OPTION requires, the report gives the most it can require: "works if the kayak tour requires swimming of one PERSON who takes part, or does not require it", or "works if brunch does not require driving a manual car". Where a larger set asks what an OPTION requires, it says that the OPTION does not require the CAPABILITY: limits that are found one at a time need not hold together. A report asks whether a PERSON has a CAPABILITY only where some OPTION of the FILE requires it or might. What an OPTION requires of an OBJECT turns out well where the OPTION does not require it, and whether it refuses an OBJECT turns out well where it does not. Where an OBJECT starts gives one unknown FACT for each PERSON and each PLACE of the FILE: that the PERSON has it, or that it lies there. A set holds at most one of them for an OBJECT. What an OPTION does with an OBJECT can help in three ways, so it gives three unknown FACTs: that the OPTION keeps the OBJECT, that it does not keep it, and that it hands it over. A set holds at most one of the three for an OPTION and an OBJECT. "Does not keep" is true of an OPTION that leaves the OBJECT alone and of one that hands it over, and a plan that counts on it uses no hand-over there. A report says "hands over" only where "does not keep" is not enough in its place. A report asks about an OBJECT only where some OPTION of the FILE requires it or refuses it, or might. A report asks for another right to an OPTION only where a FACT says that the OPTION needs a right. Where nobody knows whether it does, no set asks for another right to it: a set that asks whether the OPTION needs a right asks that it needs none. It then names each such OPTION whose rights are not COMPLETE, and says that a BOOKING for each PERSON who takes part in it is enough as well, where what those BOOKINGs charge fits the caps. Where a set asks for a price or for a BUDGET that can pay, the report says who buys which place. It reads that off one ITINERARY: the first, by rules 4 to 11 of "Which ITINERARY is shown", that works when the set turns out well and every other unknown FACT badly. For a set of one length of time, it is an ITINERARY that works at the most that length can be, or at any length where the report gives no limit. For a set of what one OPTION requires, it is an ITINERARY that works at the most the OPTION can require. For a price, the report names the PERSONs who take a place of the OFFER. For a BUDGET that can pay for a PERSON, it names each place the PERSON has to pay for, with its OFFER, and with its price where the FILE states one. Where the FILE states none, the report says that the FILE has yet to state it: the price has to be found out too, and where it is above nothing a BUDGET that lists the PERSON has to hold its currency. A place whose price the same set asks for is named with that price only. That is one way the TRIP works, and the report says so: another ITINERARY could need other purchases. The report also prints the BUDGETs of the FILE to check the purchases against: the PERSONs each lists and the currencies it holds. In a FILE with a cap, every smallest set comes with such a plan, whatever it asks: who buys which place, at what price and from which BUDGET. Under the plan comes one line for each cap: what the BOOKINGs charged to it for sure, the amount of each BOOKING that might have charged it and that the set does not ask about, what the plan buys from it, and what the cap has left after all of that. Whatever the FILE comes to state has to fit in what is left: what a BOOKING that the set asks about paid from the cap's BUDGET, the price of a place that this BUDGET pays for, what a PERSON whom no BUDGET lists has to buy if this BUDGET becomes one that pays for them, and the cost of a right or of another OPTION that is found if this BUDGET pays for it. The report says so once, in general words, since nobody can list everything a writer might find. What is left is one amount for the cap as a whole: the report does not divide it among the things that might be charged.

An unknown FACT is the start, the duration or the access cutoff of a listed OPTION, that a listed OPTION needs no right, another right to a listed OPTION for a PERSON, the price of a listed OFFER, what a listed BOOKING charged to the BUDGETs of the FILE, that a BUDGET can pay for a PERSON, what a listed OPTION requires of a CAPABILITY, that a PERSON has a CAPABILITY, that a listed OPTION does not require an OBJECT, that it does not refuse an OBJECT, that it keeps an OBJECT, that it does not keep it or that it hands it over, where an OBJECT starts, that a listed OPTION SATISFIES a WANT or that it does not, the duration of a listed WAY, other WAYs from one PLACE to another, another OPTION for a WANT and a PERSON, or another transfer of an OBJECT. A report says that an OPTION SATISFIES a WANT only where the WANT is a must for some PERSON, and that it does not only where the WANT is a must-not for some PERSON.

Other WAYs are unlisted WAYs that go straight from the first PLACE to the second. Their length of time is how long each one takes. They help only if they can be taken when the ITINERARY needs them, with a right where they need one, only if they SATISFY nothing that is a must-not for a PERSON who takes them, and only if the PERSONs who take them meet whatever they require.

Another OPTION for a WANT and a PERSON is an OPTION outside the FILE that SATISFIES the WANT, that SATISFIES nothing that is a must-not for the PERSON, and that the PERSON can take part in, with a right to it where it needs one, and with whatever it requires met. Everybody who takes part in it has each CAPABILITY that it requires of each PERSON. For each CAPABILITY that it requires of one PERSON, somebody who takes part in it from its start to its end has it, and can still do everything else the TRIP asks of them. Different PERSONs can meet different requirements. As in the best case, it is one the PERSON can take part in whatever else they do. So it helps only if the PERSON can take part in it and still do everything else the TRIP asks of them. It is one condition for one WANT and one PERSON, and a report names it only for a PERSON for whom the WANT is a must. One OPTION that is found can meet the condition for several PERSONs and for several WANTs, so the conditions are counted, and the OPTIONs are not.

The price of an OFFER turns out well where it is nothing: then each PERSON who takes one of its places can pay it. A real price helps where every PERSON whom the report names can pay it, and all the purchases fit the caps together. That a BUDGET can pay for a PERSON turns out well where each place the PERSON takes at a price above nothing is paid from a BUDGET that lists them, whatever the price is: from one BUDGET for all of them, or from several. That holds for a place whose price nobody knows as well, and no cap of the FILE limits such a BUDGET. What a BOOKING charged turns out well where it charged nothing to a BUDGET in a currency with a cap. A report asks about it only where the FILE leaves it open and a cap could turn on it: for a BOOKING with no stated payment in a FILE with a cap, and for a payment above nothing that names no BUDGET, in a currency that some cap is in. Each of these is the most helpful way the FACT can turn out, and the search uses it. A real price, a real BUDGET and a real receipt help only if the sums of the plan still hold. A report asks about funding only for a PERSON whom no BUDGET lists. It asks about a price only where some PERSON might have to pay for a place of the OFFER, and whether a BUDGET can pay for a PERSON only where that PERSON might have to pay for a place. A PERSON never pays for a place of an OPTION that a FACT says needs no right, for a place of an OPTION that a BOOKING covers them for, or for a place that costs nothing. Nobody has to pay for a place of an OPTION that has a place at no cost for each PERSON whom no BOOKING covers for it.

Another right to an OPTION for a PERSON is a right that no BOOKING in the FILE secures and that is no place of an OFFER in the FILE. Where the OPTION has places on OFFER, the words say that the right is in addition to them. It is one condition for one listed OPTION and one PERSON, and it counts as a FACT about that OPTION. A report names together the PERSONs who need a right to the same OPTION. Where places on OFFER cover some of the PERSONs who need a right, a set asks for another right for the others. Each way to pick the others is enough where the remaining PERSONs can each take a place of their own, and all of those places can be paid for together. A report lists such a set where it is among the smallest that are enough.

The unknown FACTs have a fixed order. First come the OPTIONs in the FILE's order. Each OPTION has its start, its duration and its access cutoff, then that it needs no right, then what it requires of each CAPABILITY in the FILE's order, then, for each OBJECT in the FILE's order, that it does not require the OBJECT, that it does not refuse it, that it keeps it, that it does not keep it and that it hands it over, then another right to it for each PERSON in the FILE's order, then the price of each of its OFFERs in the FILE's order, then what each of its BOOKINGs charged in the FILE's order, and then, for each WANT in the FILE's order, that it SATISFIES the WANT and that it does not. Then come the durations of the WAYs, in the FILE's order. Then come the other WAYs, in the FILE's order of their first PLACE and then of their second. Then, for each PERSON in the FILE's order, comes that they have each CAPABILITY, in the FILE's order of CAPABILITIES. Then, for each OBJECT in the FILE's order, comes where it starts: with each PERSON in the FILE's order, and then at each PLACE in the FILE's order. Then, for each PERSON in the FILE's order whom no BUDGET lists, comes that a BUDGET can pay for them. A price or a payer that a report never asks about is not among the unknown FACTs, so it has no place in the order. Then come the conditions for another OPTION, in the FILE's order of WANTs and then of PERSONs. Then comes another transfer of each OBJECT, in the FILE's order. A set lists its FACTs in that order, and the sets are listed in that order too, comparing their first FACTs first.

## The answer can come first

A check can give its answer as soon as the answer is proved, ahead of its report. The answer comes by itself, marked as unfinished. It is no report and it shows no evidence: the evidence comes with the report, when the check is done. That report is the one this page describes.

| Answer | It is proved once | The report still has to find |
|---|---|---|
| FEASIBLE | one ITINERARY, with its way to pay, works in the worst case | the ITINERARY that the fixed rule shows, what it relies on, the WARNINGs and the money |
| INFEASIBLE | nothing works in the best case | the FACTs, WANTs and DECISIONs that conflict, and whether they conflict only with the BUDGETs as the FILE lists them |
| UNDETERMINED | nothing works in the worst case, and something works in the best case | the unknown FACTs that would be enough, and whether each case is settled |

The ITINERARY that proved a TRIP FEASIBLE is not shown: it need not be the one the fixed rule shows. The answer is exact, so it is the answer of the report, and the same FILE gives it on any machine. Whoever asked can act on the answer at once, and can stop the check there.
